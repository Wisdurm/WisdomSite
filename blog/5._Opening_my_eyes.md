# OpenBSD

Don't worry, this isn't going to be a post about how I have just discovered that
OpenBSD is the best operating system, that it's objectively superior to every
other alternative in every conceivable way and that it has no problems. While I
don't currently have any gripes with it, and I think it is a really good option
for a server, I wouldn't go so heavy on the glaze.

### Some *small* glazing

I don't honestly have a whole lot to say about OpenBSD right now, but I guess in
a way that's quite a good thing. The setup process went incredibly smoothly
(apart from the minor annoyance that came with understanding how partition
tables are actually displayed in the installer), and many things later on turned
out to be noticably easier than on Debian.  

Anubis, for one, was incredibly easy to get working. I actually completely gave
up on getting it working on my original server, but with OpenBSD it literally
took like 2 minutes.  

The init system is also quite sweet. I'm no professional systemd hater (though I
am slowly going in that direction), but the fact that the init system is, from
what I can gather, just a process that executes shell scripts in /etc/rc.d, is
very logical in way that feels kind of rare. Obviously it's a little bit more
complicated, but I don't think by that much.   

And that is what is really interesting about OpenBSD. It really embodies the
Unix philosophy fully. Whether for good or bad, I don't think can be accurately
discussed on this blog, but it is nonetheless very interesting. The system feels
very "bloat free", and simple, while having exactly everything you would want.  

Scratch that about what I said regarding my gripes though, since ```pf``` is
definitely not as simple as you would hope, especially when compared to
something like ```ufw```, though I suppose that too has it's reasons.

### Wrapping up... or not

## Runtime

JUST KIDDING! I AM \***STILL**\* WORKING ON THE FFI!  

A few weeks ago I concluded that I only had a few minor features and one
experimental larger feature before I would be done with the ffi. BUT HERE I AM!
It turns out that one of those "small features" (updating pointers within passed
structs) would be *really* hard. I kept getting crashes without any idea as to
the culprit, and eventually I just decided to do some refactoring and add some
wrapper classes or some bull. The refactor just kept expanding though, and I
had to **open my eyes** to just. how. bad. my code previously was.  

I certainly didn't make it perfect now, but everything makes a little bit more
sense now, and even though as of writing this everything doesn't work yet, the
bugs I've been fixing so far seem really trivial and understandable. Overall, I
think that, disregarding the fact that I lost a sizeable amount of my humanity
when having to deal with move semantics, copy constructors, and C++ in general,
this whole refactor was quite a fun experience.  

I have **so** many ideas related to Runtime, and otherwise, that I just have not
been able to work on since the ffi has been unfinished. I've now accepted that
it's not going to be perfect (shocker), but I still want it to have basically
every feature that could reasonably be implemented.  

After v.0.11.0 (assuming I have motivation left within me) Runtime will very
quickly become an entirely different language, inspired largely (though not too
much) by my experiences with Lisp. I see a future where Runtime is actually
half-decent, even if nobody other than me will ever want to even touch it.
